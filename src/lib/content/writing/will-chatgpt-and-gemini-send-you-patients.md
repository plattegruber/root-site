Yes, some — and more than last year. As of mid-2026, 47% of patients say they've used an AI chatbot like ChatGPT or Gemini to help find a provider, up from 31% at the end of 2025. But the volume of actual clicks arriving at your website from these tools is still small, and the way to show up in them is almost entirely stuff you'd be fixing anyway: your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and a website that plainly states where you are, what you do, and which insurance you take.

I build websites for dental practices, so people ask me about this roughly weekly, usually right after someone tried to sell them an "AI optimization" package. Here's what I can actually verify.

## TL;DR

- **Patients are using AI to find providers, and it's growing fast.** 47% by mid-2026, up 16 points in nine months. It now outranks plain Google results as an influence on which provider they choose.
- **The AI is often wrong about you.** Two-thirds of patients who used AI to research a provider hit incorrect information — wrong address, hours, phone, insurance. And 60% didn't verify it.
- **The traffic is small; the intent is high.** AI referrals average around 1% of site traffic across industries, but convert at a much higher rate than ordinary search.
- **ChatGPT leans on Yelp and Google now, not Foursquare.** OpenAI licensed 330 million Yelp reviews in July 2026. A large August 2026 study found Foursquare's citation share is statistically indistinguishable from zero — despite an industry stat that says otherwise.
- **There is no separate AI checklist.** Complete profile, recent reviews, consistent contact details, clear pages that answer real questions. Same list as always.

## Are patients actually using ChatGPT and Gemini to find a dentist?

More than I expected, honestly.

The clearest number comes from rater8's 2026 Patient Choice Report: 47% of patients have used an AI chatbot to help find a provider, up from 31% nine months earlier. The demographic split is the surprising part. It's not the twenty-somethings driving it — 64% of patients aged 45 to 60 said they've done it, versus 28% of 18-to-29-year-olds. The people using AI to pick a dentist are the same people with grown-up dental problems and dental insurance.

More striking: 36% of patients said AI influenced their decision about care, which edged out plain Google search results (34%) for the first time. Family and friends (43%), insurance websites (43%), and review sites (40%) still beat both. So AI hasn't taken over. It has quietly become one of several things a patient checks.

For scale, ChatGPT reported roughly 1 billion weekly users in August 2026, and Google says Gemini passed 1 billion monthly users the same month. Different metrics, not directly comparable, but the direction is obvious.

The queries patients run are boring and predictable — "dentist near me" (55% run some version of "specialty near me"), "best dentist in [town]" (34%), a specific doctor's name (32%), "reviews for [practice]" (26%), and "dentist that takes [insurance]" (23%). Nothing exotic. These are the same searches they were typing into Google, now typed into a chat box.

## Where does ChatGPT actually get its answer about your practice?

This is where the industry advice gets unreliable, so I want to be careful.

For over a year, a specific claim has circulated in local SEO circles: that 60–70% of ChatGPT's local results come from Foursquare, so you'd better go fix your Foursquare listing. I've seen it repeated by agencies pitching dental clients.

In August 2026, Ben Fisher at Steady Demand actually tested it — 2,880 prompts across 12 metros and 12 categories, deliberately weighted toward the businesses Foursquare covers best. Foursquare's citation share came back at **0.00%** on ChatGPT's main surface, and 0.06% on an independent cross-check. One citation, to a gym in Los Angeles, out of 4,607 runs.

He also traced the original number. It came from a real study — 50 prompts, five Spanish cities, first-ranked result only, May 2025. All three of those caveats were gone within nine days of publication, and seven of eight later repetitions dropped every hedge while the number quietly got rounder and more confident.

What holds the slot instead? Yelp. It appeared in ChatGPT's structured business-card data in 95.83% of runs. That tracks with the news: on July 23, 2026, OpenAI licensed 330 million Yelp reviews and more than 8 million business listings for ChatGPT's local answers. Four weeks before the study ran. Fisher also tested whether Foursquare might be influencing answers invisibly, and found the opposite — the businesses ChatGPT names resemble **Google's** results more than Foursquare's.

So for a US dental practice in late 2026, ChatGPT's picture of you is assembled mostly from Yelp, Google, and your own website. Not Foursquare. And that will change again the next time someone signs a licensing deal, which is the actual lesson here: don't rebuild your marketing around a partnership. Partnerships have a shelf life. Your Google Business Profile doesn't.

## What about Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode?

Google's version is a different animal, and for a local business it's the more important one.

AI Overviews — the paragraph Google writes at the top of some results — now appear on more than 20% of searches. When one shows up, people click a lot less. Pew Research found users clicked through on 8% of searches with an Overview present versus 15% without, and only about 1% clicked a link _inside_ the Overview itself.

That sounds alarming until you look at which searches it eats. Overviews mostly answer informational questions: "is a root canal painful," "how much does a crown cost." The patient who was going to read that and not book anyway. The "dentist near me, I need an appointment" searcher still ends up in the map pack, because an AI paragraph can't get them an appointment.

Google's AI Mode grounds its local answers directly in Maps data — over 250 million businesses and places — which means your Google Business Profile is feeding it whether you think about it or not. Same profile, same reviews, same address consistency. You are already optimizing for Gemini's local answers if you've done the basic Business Profile work. If you haven't, that's the gap, and it isn't an AI gap. I wrote the longer version of that argument in [how dental practices get found online](https://root.site/writing/how-dental-practices-get-found-online).

One development worth knowing: Zocdoc and Google struck a deal letting patients book appointments inside Gemini. Booking is moving into the chat window. That's a longer-term shift, and it's another argument for making sure whatever directory data represents you is accurate.

## How much traffic will this actually send me?

Small, but good.

Across the industries Conductor tracked, AI referrals average about **1% of total website traffic**. That's it. Anyone telling you AI search is currently a primary patient channel for a general dental practice is ahead of the data.

The interesting part is what those visitors do when they arrive. AI referral traffic converts several times better than ordinary organic search — reported multiples vary a lot by source and I'd treat any single number skeptically, but the direction is consistent across studies. It makes sense: the AI has already answered the browsing questions, so the person who clicks through is further along. ChatGPT accounts for the large majority of that referral traffic; Gemini is climbing.

Practical read: a small stream of unusually ready patients. Worth being visible for. Not worth restructuring your practice over.

## The part that should actually worry you

Not the traffic. The accuracy.

Of the patients who used AI to research a provider, **two-thirds encountered incorrect information** — wrong office address, wrong phone number, wrong hours, wrong insurance. And 60% of them trusted the AI summary without verifying it.

Sit with that. A patient asks Gemini for a dentist in your town who takes their plan. It names you, and states hours you changed two years ago, or an insurance you dropped. Most of them won't check. They'll show up at 8am on a Friday you're closed, or call about a plan you don't take, and conclude you're disorganized.

This isn't a hallucination problem you can't touch. It's a stale-directory problem you can fix in an afternoon. The AI is repeating what it found. If your Google Business Profile, Yelp listing, and website disagree about your hours, the model picks one and states it confidently.

## What I'd actually do this month

In order of return:

1. **Make your website, Google Business Profile, and Yelp listing agree.** Name, address, phone, hours, insurance list. Identical. Boring. It is the single highest-leverage thing on this page.
2. **Claim and fill in your Yelp listing.** I've spent years telling dentists Yelp was mostly irrelevant. The OpenAI deal changed that, at least for now.
3. **Keep reviews recent.** 75% of patients won't consider a provider below 4.0 stars, and 44% won't go below 4.5. Recency beats volume for both Google and the AI models.
4. **Respond to negative reviews.** 66% of patients say a provider's response to a bad review affects their trust. That's a free trust signal most practices leave on the table.
5. **Put plain answers on your site.** A page that clearly states "we accept Delta Dental PPO, MetLife, and Cigna" is machine-readable in a way that "we work with most major insurers" is not. Specificity is what gets you quoted.
