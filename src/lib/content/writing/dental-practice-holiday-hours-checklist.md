Your front desk knows the office is closing early before Thanksgiving. The sign on the door says so. Your website still says you're open until five, Google shows the usual hours, and the appointment form promises a reply within one business day.

Which answer should a patient believe?

Holiday updates are small website jobs with several moving parts. The useful result is simple: someone checking your practice on a phone should know when you're open, what happens if they contact you while you're closed, and when normal service returns.

Here's a checklist to work through before the team leaves.

## 1. Agree on one schedule first

Write down the actual plan before anyone starts editing pages:

- The dates and times the office closes, including any early finish.
- The date and time it reopens.
- Whether anyone answers calls or reviews appointment requests during the break.
- The approved after-hours contact instructions.
- Who updates each system and who checks it afterward.

Separate patient-facing office hours from administrative coverage. A team member checking messages from home does not mean the practice is open for appointments.

Use full dates, including the year, in your working notes. “Closed Thursday and Friday” becomes ambiguous when someone copies it into next year's notice. For more than one location, write a separate schedule for each.

## 2. Put the notice where patients need it

Add a short, readable notice near the homepage's call and appointment options. Repeat the relevant information on the contact page and beside the appointment request form. Check any hours shown in the footer, too.

Keep regular hours visible, with the holiday exceptions clearly labeled. A temporary closure should not leave patients guessing what your normal week looks like.

For example, if these were the practice's actual arrangements:

> Holiday hours: Closed November 26–27, 2026. We reopen Monday, November 30, at 8 a.m. Appointment requests received during the closure will be reviewed after we return. For after-hours contact instructions, see our contact page.

Link that last sentence to the actual instructions. Don't publish it if the destination doesn't answer the question.

Use ordinary page text rather than putting the whole notice inside a holiday graphic. Check it on a phone: the dates should be readable without zooming, and the notice should not cover the call button.

## 3. Set Google's holiday hours separately

Updating your website does not finish the Google update.

Google's [Special hours guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) covers temporary changes and closures of up to six consecutive days. In your Business Profile, open **Edit profile → Hours → Special hours**, choose each affected date, and enter the hours or mark it closed. Save, then check the public listing.

Google also recommends confirming holiday hours when they match your regular schedule. Special hours require the profile to be set to **Open with main hours**.

For closures of seven or more consecutive days, or an unknown duration, Google directs businesses to use **Temporarily closed** instead. Check that distinction before a longer winter break, and assign the reopening update when you assign the closure update.

If you need to check the rest of the listing, the [Google Business Profile guide for dentists](/writing/google-business-profile-for-dentists) covers the broader work.

## 4. Make the phone tell the same story

Listen to the voicemail greeting rather than assuming someone changed it.

It should give the closure dates, reopening time, and the practice's real process for messages. If nobody checks routine messages during the break, say so. If calls go to an answering service, confirm its holiday instructions and coverage with whoever manages it.

Test the public phone number and any menu options using the practice's agreed testing process. Verify that the after-hours route reaches the intended destination. Avoid placing an unnecessary test call to a covering clinician without arranging it first.

Compare the recording with the website. “We'll call you back today” cannot sit beside “Our office is closed until Monday.”

## 5. Fix the appointment request expectation

Follow the website's appointment button all the way through the patient-facing screens. Check the form, confirmation page, and automatic acknowledgment with your team.

Look for promises that become untrue over the break:

- “We'll contact you within 24 hours.”
- “Your appointment is confirmed” when staff still need to approve it.
- Available appointment times that fall inside the closure.

A request form can stay available if it clearly explains when the team will review requests. Use a response window the office can meet, allowing for the backlog when it reopens.

If your system books confirmed appointments directly, make sure its actual availability reflects the closure. Changing the button's wording won't correct an open slot in the scheduler.

The [new patient page checklist](/writing/dental-practice-new-patient-page) explains the same request-versus-booking distinction for the rest of the year.

## 6. Be specific about after-hours contact

Have the dentist or practice manager approve this wording. A website notice should reflect the practice's established arrangements, not invent a clinical policy.

If there is a covering provider or an on-call route, verify the details and whether it serves existing patients, new patients, or both. If routine forms and voicemail are not monitored during the closure, state that beside those options.

Avoid “24/7 care” or “always here for you” unless the arrangements support that promise. A form that accepts messages overnight does not establish overnight coverage.

## 7. Assign the return to normal

Put a reopening check on one person's calendar, with a backup if they're away.

Remove the expired website notice, restore the routine voicemail greeting, check booking availability, and confirm the public Google listing matches the reopened office. Review any response-time wording changed for the break. Make sure someone owns the accumulated requests.

Then repeat the patient's journey: find the hours, tap the number, and follow the appointment link. That's a smaller version of the [dental website check](/writing/how-to-tell-if-your-dental-website-is-doing-its-job), and it's what tells you the job is finished.

## Questions

**Should we change our regular Google hours for a holiday?**

Use Special hours for a short holiday change so your regular schedule stays intact. Google recommends Temporarily closed for closures of seven or more consecutive days or an unknown duration.

**Can patients still request appointments while the office is closed?**

Yes, if the form explains when requests will be reviewed and that a request is not a confirmed appointment. Remove any automatic response promise your team cannot meet during the closure.

**What should our website say about after-hours care?**

Publish the contact instructions your practice has approved and can actually support. Explain whether messages are monitored, who the instructions apply to, and what patients should do next. Do not imply continuous coverage unless it exists.
